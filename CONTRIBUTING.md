\# Contributing



Thanks for your interest in contributing. Contributions are welcome, but entirely

optional.



AMPL-1.0 is designed to encourage voluntary collaboration without requiring it.

You are free to use the license or any AMPL-licensed software without contributing

changes back.



This document explains how to contribute in a way that is clear and low-friction.



---



\## 1. Ways to Contribute



You can contribute in many forms, including:



\- bug reports

\- documentation improvements

\- examples and adoption guidance

\- code changes

\- tests

\- clarifications (especially around real-world usage)



---



\## 2. Contribution Is Voluntary



Submitting contributions is never required.



\- You may keep your own modifications private.

\- You may distribute your own derived works under any terms you choose.

\- You may license or sell your own contributions separately, whether or not you

&nbsp; submit them upstream.



---



\## 3. What Happens When You Submit a Contribution



By submitting a contribution to this repository (via pull request, patch, or

equivalent), you agree that:



1\. You have the right to submit the material, and

2\. You grant permission for the maintainers to use, modify, and redistribute

&nbsp;  \*\*the submitted material\*\* under terms compatible with AMPL-1.0.



This permission applies only to what you submit. It does not affect:

\- your other projects,

\- your non-submitted work,

\- or any separate licensing you choose for your own code.



This approach is intended to keep contribution legally simple and to avoid

surprises.



---



\## 4. Attribution and Credit



If you contribute:



\- You may add your name, handle, or preferred attribution in the commit metadata

&nbsp; and/or in relevant file headers if appropriate.

\- Existing author attributions must not be removed or altered to obscure authorship.



Attribution recognizes origin and does not imply endorsement.



---



\## 5. Style and Practical Guidelines



\### 5.1 Documentation changes

\- Keep wording neutral and non-ideological.

\- Avoid adding new “requirements” that are not in `LICENSE.txt`.

\- Prefer clarity over breadth.



\### 5.2 License text changes

\- `LICENSE.txt` is considered canonical and stable for AMPL-1.0.

\- Changes to `LICENSE.txt` will not be accepted except to correct clear copying

&nbsp; errors in the repository (if any exist). Substantive changes require a new

&nbsp; version number (e.g., AMPL-1.1 or AMPL-2.0).



\### 5.3 Examples and comparisons

\- Examples must be explicitly labeled as non-binding.

\- Comparisons should be descriptive rather than persuasive.



---



\## 6. Reporting Issues



When opening an issue, please include:



\- what you expected to happen

\- what actually happened

\- steps to reproduce (if applicable)

\- relevant environment details (platform, compiler, version, etc.)



For documentation suggestions:

\- quote the exact section you are referring to

\- propose replacement text if possible



---



\## 7. Submitting Pull Requests



\- Keep changes focused.

\- If you are making documentation edits, prefer small, reviewable commits.

\- If you are changing examples, ensure they remain platform-neutral unless the

&nbsp; file is explicitly about historical context.



---



\## 8. Code of Conduct



This project follows a simple rule:



Be respectful, be constructive, and assume good faith.



The goal is clarity and usability, not ideology or confrontation.
