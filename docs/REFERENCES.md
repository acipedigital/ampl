\# References \& Suggested Reading



The following materials provide historical, social, economic, and technical

context for the Amiga ecosystem, the demo scene, and broader questions of

software preservation and licensing culture.



These references are provided for educational background and do not constitute

legal authority.



---



\## Amiga History \& Culture



\- Jimmy Maher — \*The Future Was Here: The Commodore Amiga\*  

&nbsp; A comprehensive history of the Amiga platform, its technology, and its

&nbsp; community.



\- Brian Bagnall — \*On the Edge: The Spectacular Rise and Fall of Commodore\*  

&nbsp; Industry and economic context surrounding Commodore and its products.



\- Amiga Format, CU Amiga, and Amiga World magazine archives  

&nbsp; Primary sources documenting Amiga-era development practices, culture, and

&nbsp; commercial software.



---



\## Demo Scene \& Creative Computing



\- Tasajärvi et al. — \*Demoscene: The Art of the Algorithms\*  

&nbsp; Cultural and technical history of the demo scene, including attribution

&nbsp; practices and informal collaboration.



\- Pouët.net  

&nbsp; Archive and documentation of demo productions and scene culture.



\- Scene.org  

&nbsp; Long-running archive preserving demos, tools, and scene history.



---



\## Software Preservation \& Archival Practice



\- Internet Archive — Software Collections  

&nbsp; Large-scale efforts to preserve historical software and computing artifacts.



\- Software Preservation Network (SPN)  

&nbsp; Research and advocacy around legal and technical preservation challenges.



\- Digital Preservation Coalition  

&nbsp; Reports on long-term digital preservation strategies.



\- Jason Scott — talks and writings on software archaeology  

&nbsp; Practical insights into preserving abandoned and orphaned software.



---



\## Software Licensing \& Culture



\- Eric S. Raymond — \*The Cathedral and the Bazaar\*  

&nbsp; Early analysis of different software development models.



\- Open Source Initiative (OSI) — License taxonomy and documentation  

&nbsp; Reference material on common license categories and definitions.



\- SPDX License List documentation  

&nbsp; Standards and practices for machine-readable license identification.



---



\## Historical Context \& Commentary



\- Interviews and writings by Amiga-era developers in \*Coders at Work\*

\- Contemporary discussions on software longevity, abandonware, and digital

&nbsp; heritage preservation



These materials help explain why different licensing models exist and why

software ecosystems have historically succeeded through a variety of social

and legal structures.
