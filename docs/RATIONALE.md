\# Why AMPL Exists: Freedom, Credit, and Software That Lasts



\## Introduction



Software licensing is often treated as a purely legal matter, but in practice it

shapes ecosystems, communities, and long-term survivability.



AMPL exists to formalize a permissive model of software sharing that emphasizes:



\- freedom of use,

\- recognition of authorship upon redistribution,

\- voluntary contribution,

\- commercial coexistence,

\- and explicit support for preservation.



AMPL is not designed to enforce ideology. It is designed to reduce friction and

maximize long-term utility.



---



\## Freedom Without Guilt



AMPL grants broad rights to use, modify, redistribute, and sublicense software

for any purpose, including commercial purposes.



It imposes no obligation to publish modifications, disclose source code, or adopt

AMPL for derivative works.



Freedom under AMPL is unconditional.



---



\## Credit as Identity, Not Control



AMPL requires attribution upon redistribution.



This attribution requirement is not intended as marketing or enforcement leverage.

It exists to preserve recognition of origin.



In many historical software cultures, attribution served as a social signal of respect,

reputation, and lineage. AMPL reflects that reality by requiring that existing author

acknowledgments be preserved.



Attribution under AMPL does not imply endorsement or involvement in later modifications.



---



\## Voluntary Collaboration



AMPL explicitly allows contribution back to original projects, but does not require it.



If a recipient chooses to contribute improvements upstream, they may do so without

surrendering control of unrelated work. AMPL clarifies that submitted contributions

grant permission only for the submitted material.



If a recipient chooses not to contribute back, nothing happens. Rights are not revoked.

No penalties apply.



---



\## Commerce Is Normal



AMPL permits commercial and closed-source use without restriction.



Developers may sell software using AMPL-licensed code. They may also license their own

contributions separately, including for compensation.



This reflects a practical reality: sustainable software ecosystems often involve both

commercial and non-commercial development.



---



\## Preservation Matters



Many licenses are written as if projects will always have active maintainers and

reachable authors. In practice, software is frequently abandoned, orphaned, or

outlives the organization that created it.



AMPL explicitly permits preservation and archival redistribution to ensure that software

may survive beyond its original distribution environment.



---



\## Platform Neutrality



AMPL is platform-neutral. It is not restricted to any operating system, emulator,

hardware, or future platform.



While it draws inspiration from early personal computing and demo-scene cultures,

including those associated with the Amiga era, these references are historical and

cultural only.



---



\## What AMPL Avoids



AMPL intentionally avoids features that increase friction or uncertainty:



\- mandatory source disclosure

\- license inheritance or copyleft

\- network-use provisions

\- termination traps

\- moral or ideological enforcement



AMPL is designed to be minimal, clear, and usable in real-world mixed environments.



---



\## Conclusion



AMPL exists to let software live, evolve, and move freely across platforms and projects,

while remembering who started it.



It preserves freedom without guilt, credit without control, commerce without shame, and

collaboration without coercion.
