\# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)



This document answers common questions about the Acipe Minimal Permissive

License (AMPL-1.0) in practical, non-ideological terms.



---



\## Why does AMPL exist?



AMPL exists to provide a permissive software license that emphasizes:



\- unconditional freedom of use,

\- persistent recognition of authorship,

\- voluntary collaboration rather than enforced reciprocity,

\- compatibility with commercial and closed-source development,

\- and explicit support for long-term preservation.



It is inspired by earlier software cultures that successfully balanced sharing,

commerce, and personal authorship without coercive licensing models.



---



\## Why not GPL?



GPL licenses are designed to enforce reciprocity by requiring derivative works

to disclose source code and inherit the same license terms.



For some projects, this is intentional and appropriate. For others, it introduces

legal ambiguity, discourages reuse, complicates commercial adoption, and creates

friction around preservation and long-term maintenance.



AMPL takes a different approach. It does not attempt to shape downstream behavior

through obligation or enforcement. Instead, it maximizes freedom of use and treats

contribution as voluntary.



AMPL does not oppose GPL; it serves developers who want a non-copyleft alternative.



---



\## Why not MIT or BSD?



MIT and BSD licenses are widely used and highly successful permissive licenses.



AMPL builds on the permissive model but makes several expectations explicit that

MIT/BSD leave implicit or unspecified:



\- Attribution is required upon redistribution and explicitly survives closed or

&nbsp; commercial use.

\- Voluntary contribution back is clearly permitted and legally safe.

\- Preservation and archival redistribution are explicitly allowed.

\- Author identity may include handles or pseudonyms, not only legal names.

\- Closed-source use is explicitly normalized rather than assumed.



AMPL exists to clarify these expectations in the license text itself, rather than

relying on convention or interpretation.



---



\## Is AMPL only for Amiga or retro projects?



No.



AMPL is platform-neutral. It places no restrictions on hardware, operating

systems, emulators, reimplementations, or future platforms.



The Amiga influence is cultural and historical, not technical. You do not need

to know anything about the Amiga to use AMPL.



---



\## Can I sell software that uses AMPL-licensed code?



Yes.



AMPL explicitly permits commercial use, closed-source redistribution, and

separate licensing of your own original work, including for compensation.



There are no royalties, fees, or revenue-sharing requirements.



---



\## Do I have to contribute changes back?



No.



Contribution is welcome but never required. Choosing not to contribute back does

not limit, revoke, or condition any rights granted under the license.



---



\## If I do contribute back, what happens?



If you voluntarily submit contributions:



\- You grant permission only for the material you submit.

\- You retain full rights to all non-submitted work.

\- You may still license or sell your work separately.



Submitting contributions does not impose any obligation on your other projects.



---



\## What does “attribution” mean under AMPL?



Attribution means preserving recognition of the original author(s) when software

is redistributed.



\- If source code is distributed, existing author notices in the source must

&nbsp; remain intact.

\- If only binaries are distributed, attribution must be provided in accompanying

&nbsp; documentation, credits, or a NOTICE file.



Attribution recognizes origin only and does not imply endorsement or involvement

in later modifications.



---



\## Does AMPL require attribution at runtime?



No.



AMPL does not require splash screens, startup messages, or UI branding.



Attribution may be satisfied through documentation or equivalent materials.



---



\## Does AMPL grant trademark rights?



No.



AMPL applies to software code only. Names, logos, and branding are not licensed

unless explicitly stated otherwise by the author.



---



\## Is AMPL compatible with other licenses?



AMPL is non-viral and does not impose downstream licensing requirements.



It can be used alongside proprietary software and permissive open-source licenses.

Copyleft licenses may impose their own requirements on combined works independently

of AMPL.



AMPL itself does not impose copyleft obligations.



---



\## Is AMPL suitable for enterprise or institutional use?



Yes.



AMPL is designed to minimize legal and operational friction. It contains no

termination clauses, patent clauses, network-use provisions, or mandatory

disclosure requirements.



Its risk profile is comparable to MIT/BSD licenses, with clearer attribution and

preservation language.



---



\## Is AMPL approved by OSI or listed by SPDX?



AMPL is a new license authored in 2026.



SPDX and OSI recognition may be pursued, but AMPL does not depend on institutional

approval to be used. Until SPDX listing is complete, the identifier

`LicenseRef-AMPL-1.0` should be used.



---



\## In one sentence



AMPL allows software to be used, reused, sold, preserved, and shared freely —

while remembering who started it.
