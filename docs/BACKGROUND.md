\# Background: Why AMPL Draws From Earlier Software Ecosystems



The Acipe Minimal Permissive License (AMPL-1.0) is a permissive license designed

to allow broad use of software while preserving recognition of original authorship

upon redistribution.



AMPL draws inspiration from earlier software ecosystems that successfully balanced

reuse, commerce, personal reputation, and informal collaboration before modern

centralized platforms became dominant.



One of the most notable examples of such an ecosystem was the era of early personal

computing, including the cultural environment that formed around the Amiga platform

and the demo scene.



---



\## Software Culture Before Centralized Infrastructure



Many modern assumptions about software development are relatively new:



\- global collaboration platforms

\- automated compliance tooling

\- institutional open source governance

\- long-lived, centrally maintained repositories



Earlier software communities were often smaller, decentralized, and dependent on

physical distribution. In that environment, sharing and reuse were frequently driven

by practical necessity rather than ideology.



---



\## Attribution as Social Identity



In earlier computing cultures, authorship was often expressed through:



\- real names

\- handles

\- group names

\- scene aliases



Credit was part of reputation and community identity. Attribution was expected

socially even when it was not enforced legally.



AMPL reflects this reality by explicitly supporting attribution that includes handles,

pseudonyms, or group identities.



---



\## Commerce and Sharing Coexisting



Commercial software, freeware, shareware, and informal distribution commonly

coexisted. Developers often built tools, libraries, and applications in a mixed

environment where selling software did not imply hostility to sharing.



AMPL explicitly supports this coexistence by permitting closed-source redistribution

and commercial use without forcing disclosure or license inheritance.



---



\## Preservation as a First-Class Concern



Software often outlives its platforms, companies, and sometimes its authors.

Preservation and archival distribution are essential to preventing the loss of

software history and technical knowledge.



AMPL explicitly permits preservation-oriented redistribution, including distribution

through disk images, firmware images, emulation, and other forms appropriate to the

platform.



---



\## Platform Neutrality



Although AMPL draws inspiration from early personal computing communities, including

those associated with the Amiga era, it is not restricted to any platform, technology,

or community.



The Amiga influence is cultural and contextual, not technical or normative.



AMPL is intended to be usable by modern and future software projects on any platform.
